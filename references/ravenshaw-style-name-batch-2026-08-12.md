# Ravenshaw-Style British Surname Brand Batch — Draft 1

> **Reference style:** The user likes the character of `Ravenshaw`, not necessarily the name itself. This batch therefore uses English-looking, surname-style constructions with strong consonants, an implied landscape or estate history, and enough dramatic shape to feel like a premium British performance marque.

## Design Criteria

The candidate must sound natural to a British audience without being a generic finance word, a Latin-styled invention, a number construction, or a conventional investment-firm partnership. It should be distinctive enough to carry the platform brand on its own, with the factual descriptor **“AI stock signals and paper-trading tools.”**

## Screening Scope

The initial public-web screen will assess exact and close uses in UK and international finance, investment, trading, fintech, market data, technology, and regulated categories. It is not trademark, company-name, domain, social-handle, or legal clearance.

## Refined Shortlist

| Candidate | Brand reading |
|---|---|
| **Blackmere** | The boldest and most distinctive; dark, premium, and visually strong. |
| **Thornwick** | The most energetic; English in feel, sharp in silhouette, and well suited to a modern performance platform. |
| **Hartmoor** | The most classically British; quieter, confident, and closest to a heritage performance marque. |
| **Foxwell** | The most approachable; refined and intelligent without feeling formal. |
| **Greymark** | The most modern; precise, scalable, and still compatible with the British performance tone. |

## Provisional Recommendation

**Thornwick** is the strongest overall creative candidate: it retains the surname-like British character that made Ravenshaw appealing, has more energy than Hartmoor, and is less generic than Foxwell. **Blackmere** is the bolder alternative if the brand should feel darker and more premium.
